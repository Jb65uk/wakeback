WakeBack build guide

Building the first puck on the carrier board, from ordering the PCB to a real track on the viewer. No electronics knowledge needed beyond being able to solder. Do the steps in order: each one ends with a test, so if something's wrong you know exactly which bit.

This is the first build. The board and the test firmware have been checked on the computer but not yet built for real. Treat puck one as the prototype, and expect to find a thing or two to change.

What you're building

One round board with everything wired to it. The small computer (XIAO) and the heel sensor sit on top. The charger module sits underneath, the battery below that, and the wireless charging coil under the battery. The GPS goes in the lid, facing the sky, on four wires.

Order the boards

Shipping: DHL is about £30. Pick the cheapest economy option at checkout instead (usually called Global Standard Direct Line or similar): a few pounds, and about 2 weeks.

Yes, you just send it. The file wakeback-carrier-v1-gerbers.zip is everything a PCB factory needs. Don't unzip it: upload the zip as it is.

  1. Go to jlcpcb.com and make an account.
  2. Click Order now, then Add gerber file, and pick the zip.
  3. After a few seconds it shows a picture of the board and fills in the size: it should say 60 × 60 mm and 2 layers. Click the preview to check it looks like the pictures above.
  4. Check the settings below. Most are already right.
  5. Save to cart, then Checkout. Pick a shipping option: the cheapest takes a couple of weeks, DHL/FedEx about a week.

Let JLCPCB fit the resistors and LED (recommended)

The tiny parts are the fiddliest bit to hand-solder, and they're all JLCPCB "basic" parts, so it's cheap to have them fitted. Everything else (XIAO, modules, battery socket, wires) you still solder yourself.

  1. On the same order page, switch PCB Assembly on. Choose Economic, Top Side, PCBA Qty 2 (the other 3 boards come bare). Tooling holes: Added by JLCPCB. Click Confirm.
  2. Upload wakeback-carrier-v1-BOM.csv as the BOM and wakeback-carrier-v1-CPL.csv as the CPL (pick-and-place) file, then Process BOM & CPL.
  3. It should find three parts, all ticked: C17900 (100 kΩ ×4), C17887 (300 Ω) and C84256 (red LED). If one says out of stock, pick any other 1206 resistor of the same value, or any 0805 LED, from the basic list.
  4. On the placement preview, zoom in on the LED. Its cathode mark (the little line or dot) should sit on the pad labelled K, toward the right. If it's the wrong way round, select it and rotate it 180°. Resistors don't matter.
  5. Carry on to the cart. Expect roughly £8–10 extra for assembly (a setup fee plus pennies for the parts).

Expect about £5 for the boards plus shipping. UK VAT is normally added at checkout on small orders, so there's nothing extra to pay on delivery. It'll ask you to confirm the order after their engineers check the files, usually within a few hours.

Tools

  • Soldering iron with a fine tip (330–350 °C), solder, and a flux pen
  • Fine tweezers, for the tiny resistors
  • Multimeter: for checking the battery's + and −, and for the continuity checks
  • Foam double-sided tape, and a USB-C data cable

Soldering tips

The tiny parts (resistors and LED)

They look fiddly but they're the easiest thing on the board once you know the trick:

  1. Put a small blob of solder on one of the two pads.
  2. Hold the part with tweezers, heat that blob, and slide the part into it. Take the iron away and it's held.
  3. Solder the other end normally.
  4. Go back and touch the first end again with a dab of fresh solder so it flows properly.

Wires

  • Strip about 2 mm, twist, and tin the end (coat it with solder) before it goes near the board.
  • Push the wire through the pad's hole from the side the module is on, and solder on the other side. Snip the spare.
  • Keep wires short and neat: just long enough to reach, plus a little slack.
LiPo battery safety. Before you plug the battery into anything, check its + and − with the multimeter. Cheap batteries often have the red and black swapped in the plug, and a backwards battery can destroy the charger or make the battery swell. Never short the leads, and never leave it charging unattended on the bench.

Set up the computer

You'll use this to put the test program on the XIAO and to see what it reports.

  1. Install VS Code from code.visualstudio.com.
  2. In VS Code, click the Extensions icon on the left (four squares), search PlatformIO IDE, and install it. It takes a few minutes and may ask to restart.
  3. Unzip wakeback.zip somewhere, then File → Open Folder… and pick wakeback/firmware/bench_test (that folder, not the whole project).
  4. Wait until the blue bar at the bottom shows a ✓ tick and a → arrow. That means it's ready.

You'll use three buttons on that blue bar:

  • ✓ Build makes sure the program compiles.
  • → Upload puts it on the XIAO.
  • Plug icon: Serial Monitor shows what the XIAO is saying. Type commands at the bottom and press Enter.
Check: click ✓ Build now, before any hardware arrives. The first time it downloads its tools (several minutes). It should end with SUCCESS. If it shows an error instead, send it to me: the test program hasn't been built on a real machine yet, and a small fix may be needed.

2

XIAO

You need: the XIAO, two 7-pin female headers, a short red and a short black wire (about 60 mm each).

  1. First, the battery wires. Turn the XIAO over. Near one end there are two small pads labelled BAT+ and BAT− (look for the + and −). Solder the red wire to BAT+ and the black to BAT−. Do this now, because once the XIAO is fitted you can't reach them.
  2. Push the two female headers into the board's 14 XIAO holes from the top. Solder one pin of each underneath, check they're straight, then do the rest.
  3. Plug the XIAO into the headers with its USB socket at the board edge, matching the "USB <" marking.
  4. Leave the two battery wires loose for now. They go on in step 6.
Orientation matters. If the XIAO goes in the wrong way round, nothing will work and the power pins will be in the wrong place. The USB socket must point at the board edge.
3

First power-up

  1. Plug the XIAO into the computer with the USB-C cable.
  2. In VS Code click → Upload. Wait for SUCCESS.
  3. Click the plug icon to open the Serial Monitor. Press the XIAO's tiny R (reset) button to see it start.

Upload fails? Unplug the USB, hold the XIAO's B (boot) button, plug back in, let go, and upload again. You only need to do this the first time.

4

GPS

You need: the BE-880 GPS and its wires. It'll live in the lid, so give the wires enough length to reach (about 80 mm) and cut the rest off.

Only four of its six wires are used. Go by the labels printed on the GPS board, not the wire colours: they vary between sellers.

The pads are named from the GPS's side: the GPS's TX wire goes to the pad marked TX.

{{GPS}}
  1. Press the XIAO's R button with the monitor open.
  2. Take the board near a window or outside, with the GPS's flat ceramic square facing the sky.
Check: it says GPS: found at 38400 baud, set to 10 Hz, sea mode. Within a couple of minutes outside, the status line shows 3D fix, 10 or more sats, 10 Hz and under 3 m accuracy. Indoors it may never get a fix, and that's normal.
5

Heel sensor

You need: the BNO085, five short wires, foam tape.

The BNO085 has lots of labelled pins. You only use five: VIN GND SDA SCL INT. Leave the rest empty.

  1. Solder the five wires to the BNO085 first, on the side opposite its chip.
  2. Stick it inside the outline on the board with foam tape, square to the outline. Heel is measured from it, so it mustn't wobble.
  3. Solder the wires to the IMU pads.
{{IMU}}
  1. Press R. It should say IMU: found.
  2. Put the board flat on the table and type z Enter. Heel and pitch now read about 0.
  3. Tip the board the way the boat heels to starboard. Heel should go positive.
    • If tipping sideways changes pitch instead, type a, lay it flat, and z again.
    • If heel goes negative, type f.
Check: flat reads 0, tipped to starboard reads plus, tipped nose-up gives plus pitch. The puck remembers these settings.
6

Battery and charger

You need: the TP4056 charger, the JST-PH socket, the battery, six short wires, the multimeter. Unplug the USB cable before starting this step.

  1. Battery socket. Solder the JST-PH socket into the two holes marked BATT, from underneath (the battery is below the board). The square pad is +.
  2. Check the battery. Meter on DC volts, probes into the back of the battery's plug. The pin that reads + (about 3.7–4.2 V with red probe on it) must line up with the square pad when plugged in. If it's backwards, don't plug it in: swap the wires in the plug or get a different battery.
  3. Charger. Stick the TP4056 underneath, in its outline, USB end to the board edge. Wire its six pads to the six pads with the same names.
  4. XIAO battery wires. The red wire from step 2 goes to BAT+ and the black to BAT− in the bottom row.
{{POWER}}
Before plugging the battery in, three meter checks (continuity / beep setting, battery unplugged):
  • 3V3 pad to GND pad: no beep
  • OUT+ to OUT−: no beep
  • IN− to OUT−: no beep. They're kept apart on purpose: the charger's protection sits between them.
Any beep means a solder bridge somewhere. Find it before going on.
  1. Plug in the battery. The board's LED should start blinking by itself: it's running on battery.
  2. Plug the USB cable back in and open the monitor.
Check: the status line shows batt 3.9x V nn%, within about 0.05 V of what the meter reads across the battery.
7

Wireless charging

You need: the Qi receiver and coil, the Qi pad.

{{QI}}
  1. Wire the Qi receiver's + / 5V to Qi 5V and its − / GND to Qi GND.
  2. Unplug the USB (don't charge from both at once).
  3. Lay the coil flat and centred on the Qi pad.
Check: the TP4056's red LED lights (charging). With the USB back in, the monitor says on the pad, charging. Lift the coil off and it says off the pad. When full, the TP4056 LED goes blue or green.

If the pad doesn't start: centre the coil and try again. Some pads are fussy with small coils, so try another pad if you have one.

8

First track

  1. In the monitor type p1 Enter (this is puck 1), then r Enter to start recording.
  2. Unplug the USB. The LED double-blinks while it records.
  3. Put it in a food box with the GPS facing up, and go for a walk, a bike ride, or better, a sail. It records whenever it has a fix, up to about 3 hours.
  4. Back home, plug in and close the Serial Monitor (only one program can talk to it at once).
  5. In VS Code open a terminal (Terminal → New Terminal) in the wakeback folder and run:
pip install pyserial
python tools/pull_track.py
Check: it saves a file like puck1_103000.csv. Open wakeback/viewer/index.html in a browser and drag the file onto the map. That's your first real track.
